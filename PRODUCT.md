# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two audiences inside one portal. (1) Practice staff at small-to-mid behavioral health and medical practices: office managers and practice admins juggling credentialing alongside a dozen other jobs, not technical, often stressed about payer deadlines. (2) Lanyard's own credentialing team (admin + lanyard_staff): in the app all day, working across every practice, moving fast through queues of enrollments, documents, and follow-ups. The job to be done is always "get and keep providers enrolled with payers without anything falling through the cracks."

## Product Purpose

Lanyard Health automates provider credentialing and payer enrollment: CAQH sync, document collection, enrollment tracking, re-attestation reminders, and payer submissions. Success looks like a practice trusting the portal enough to stop keeping their own spreadsheet, and Lanyard's internal team running every practice's work from one queue.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, warm. Healthcare-grade steadiness with genuine reassurance: the interface should feel like a competent person who has it handled, not a machine and not a cheerleader. Unhurried, clean, quietly friendly. Emotional goal for practices: relief ("someone is on this"). For internal staff: clarity and momentum.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboard: walls of identical stat cards, gradient buttons, purple-on-white startup look.
- Clinical/EHR software (Epic, eClinicalWorks): gray, cramped, 2010-era enterprise density and chrome.

## Design Principles

1. **Nothing falls through the cracks, visibly.** Overdue, expiring, and at-risk items are always the loudest thing on a screen; the design's job is triage.
2. **Reassure, don't decorate.** Warmth comes from copy, whitespace, and soft edges, never from ornament or gradients.
3. **One green, used with intent.** The deep Lanyard green marks primary actions and identity; status colors do the semantic work.
4. **Earned familiarity.** Standard patterns (tables, tabs, badges) executed with unusual care beat novel UI. The tool disappears into the task.
5. **Plain English everywhere.** Labels and empty states written for a non-technical office manager; no jargon, no system-speak.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Status is never color-alone (badges carry text/dots). Respect reduced motion. Keyboard-reachable actions; the app already ships a command palette for power users.
